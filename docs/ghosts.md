# Ghosts — appearance and animation

What a ghost looks like, why the afterimage shader works the way it does, and how the walk is scrubbed. Possession is a separate document: `ghost-possession-design.md`.

---

## Ghosts (`Assets/Scripts/Ghost/`)

`PlayerRecorder` records position, yaw and a **bitmask of interaction signals** at a fixed interval; `GhostReplayer` scrubs the timeline and reports those signals back. **Ghosts have no collider at all**, so they can never trigger anything by touching it.

- `GhostInteractable` is the abstraction: `PlayerSignal` (sampled into one bit) and `SetGhostSignal` (fed back an iteration later). `FloorButton` and `Drawer` are the implementations.
- **`ghostInteractables` is `{ floorButton, drawer, pad3A, pad3B, pad3C, pad3D }`.** Bit position is the index, and `PlayerRecorder.interactables` must be the same array in the same order — `SceneBuilder` builds one and hands it to both. **Append, never reorder**: reordering invalidates every timeline recorded so far. (It is only safe to have removed the door button because timelines live for one session and are never persisted.)
- **Signals are levels, not events.** A ghost advances by elapsed time and can skip several recorded frames in one tick, so a one-frame pulse would eventually be missed. Press-type interactables stretch their pulse (`Drawer.openPulseDuration` 0.15s) and act on the rising edge at replay.
- **Only the real player's actions may raise a signal.** `Drawer.RegisterPlayerOpen` sets the pulse; a ghost's replayed pull goes straight to `Open()`. Route a ghost through the recording path and each iteration inherits the last one's actions — the drawer would open earlier and earlier until it opened on frame one.
- **A ghost releases every signal and hides itself once its timeline runs out** (`GhostReplayer.Tick`, guarded by `finished`). Clamping at the last frame — what the scrub loop does on its own — keeps applying that frame's signals forever. Harmless while every timeline was 60 seconds, decisive once `EndCycleControl` made short ones possible: **quit at t=5 standing on the pad and that signal would hold from t=5 to t=60 of every future iteration**, five seconds buying a 55-second hold. With the release in place, deciding when to quit *is* deciding how long your past self keeps standing there. **`EndCycleControl` only works because of this.**
- **Ghosts are hidden for the whole wake-up** (`SetVisible`, driven from `RunLoop`). Every timeline's frame 0 is the bed spawn, so `ResetPlayback` parks *every ghost ever made* on the spot the player is about to open their eyes on. They return exactly as the clock starts, which is also the frame they start moving, so they appear already walking.
### What a ghost looks like

**Ghosts are rigged figures rendered as afterimages** — a hollow, cool-white silhouette that walks, not the six faint primitives that came before and not the opaque person that sat between them. The rig is what carries the reading: the *shape* is a person who was here, while the *surface* is a residue the room left behind. Six of them is still a crowd, but a crowd you can see the room through.

- The model is **Quaternius' Smooth Male Casual (CC0)**, `Assets/ArtAssets/Smooth_Male_Casual@Walking.fbx` — 7,932 triangles, 31 bones, twelve clips baked in.
- **Scale 0.377**, measured not guessed: the rig stands 4.739m at scale 1 (bone extents, verified in the editor), so this puts it at 1.75m — just under the player's 1.8m controller and right for the 1.6m eye height it is seen from.
- **Still no colliders** — a ghost is something you walk *through*, and one that pushed the player would change the recording being made against it.

#### `Assets/Shaders/GhostFaint.shader` — why the alpha comes from fresnel

The figures were briefly **opaque people**, on the reasoning that the reference film shows past selves as people. Played, that turned out to be too much information: seven photoreal-ish figures in a white box read as a room full of strangers, not as your own past. **The fix is not lower alpha.** Dropping a lit material's alpha changes nothing about the *detail* — every crease and feature is still there, the whole figure is merely dimmer, and the silhouette dims with it. What was wanted is the opposite: **lose the interior, keep the outline.**

So alpha is `lerp(_CoreAlpha, _RimAlpha, pow(1 - dot(N,V), _RimPower))`. A surface turned toward the eye falls to 0.06 and takes its detail with it; the grazing band around the silhouette rises to 0.75. The figure comes out hollow with its edge intact, and since the edge is recomputed from the skinned normals every frame, **the walk still reads perfectly** — which is the whole reason the rig survived the change.

- **This is the first hand-written shader in the project.** ShaderGraph was not an option: it cannot be authored from a script, and `SceneBuilder` being the source of truth is not negotiable. Tuning values live in `GhostFaintMaterial()`, not in the shader's `Properties` defaults — the shader is the mechanism, `SceneBuilder` is the value, same as everywhere else.
- **A real blur was considered and rejected**, and the reason is worth keeping: gaussian blur destroys the silhouette first. It is the *opposite* of the requirement. It would also merge Room3's four converging ghosts into one smear, and that convergence is the intended climax of the run.
- **Unlit.** The balloons are on URP/Lit because they are real objects that take the ceiling fixtures; an afterimage is lit by nothing, and running it through the light loop would only make it flicker as it walks under each downlight.
- **One material across all six submeshes**, where the opaque version cloned six (`Ghost{Skin,Shirt,Pants,Hair,Eyes,Socks}.mat`). Tinting them apart would survive the fresnel and hand back exactly what the change removes — a rim that still says shirt-here, trousers-there is a legible person, just a see-through one. The old six are left on disk, unreferenced; going back means pointing at them again.
- **`ZWrite Off`**, or a ghost would occlude the ghosts behind it and Room3's four would punch holes in each other. The cost is that overlapping ghosts accumulate alpha, which is what keeps `_RimAlpha` below 1.
- **`_BottomFade` 0.35m dissolves the feet.** A hard edge where a figure meets the floor reads as a person standing on it. It is measured from `UNITY_MATRIX_M._m13` (the renderer's world origin, which sits at the feet) rather than from object-space Y, which would have to be un-scaled by the 0.377 by hand and would break silently the day that scale moves.
- **`_SoftFade` needs `_CameraDepthTexture`, so `ConfigureLightingPipeline` now sets `m_RequireDepthTexture`.** SSAO happens to request depth as a pass input today, which is exactly the kind of accident that breaks the day the renderer feature is retuned — the dependency is declared rather than inherited.
- **Shadows are OFF again**, reversing the call made when the ghosts were opaque ("a person with no shadow reads as a bug"). It does not survive the change: a hollow rim throwing a solid, fully detailed shadow puts every piece of detail this shader exists to remove straight back into the room, in the one place the eye reads as ground truth. `updateWhenOffscreen` stays on — the bind-pose bounds this rig reports are wrong enough to cull ghosts that are plainly in view.
- **Worst case is a ghost against a white wall**, and it was checked: still legible, but the faintest it gets. If it ever needs more presence, raise `_RimAlpha` before touching `_RimPower` — below ~1.5 the whole figure hazes over and the silhouette stops being a line, above ~4 the rim thins to a wire and reads as an outline filter rather than a body.
- **Every clip is SCRUBBED, never played.** `animator.speed` is pinned at 0 and `GhostReplayer` writes the normalized time itself each frame. That is what preserves the property the primitive limbs had: **the walk advances with distance travelled, not with the clock**, so stride length is constant instead of the legs spinning faster the quicker the ghost moves. Only position and yaw are recorded, so the walk has to be inferred, and distance is the only honest way to infer it.
  - **A TURN IS DISTANCE.** Yaw was recorded from the start and nothing was ever driven from it, so a ghost standing on a pad and looking around played the idle clip while its whole body rotated rigidly — a statue on a turntable, and in Room3 four of them do it for forty seconds at a stretch. The fix is not a turn-in-place clip (this rig has none, and it is **Generic**, so nothing external retargets onto it): `travel = distance + |Δyaw| · turnRadius`, one number feeding the same phase. **`turnRadius` 0.2m** is roughly the circle the feet trace shuffling round on the spot, about half a stance width.
    - Adding the two rather than choosing between them is the point: **walking a curve is both at once**, and it wants a slightly longer stride than the straight-line position delta measures — which is exactly what falls out. No state to pick, no blend, nothing to fall out of sync.
    - **`walkThreshold` doubles as the dead zone for turning**, and the numbers land well: a 20°/s look-around comes to 0.07 m/s and stays idle, 45°/s to 0.157 and stays idle, a deliberate 90°/s turn to 0.314 and shuffles. Casual mouse-looking does not make a ghost paddle.
    - **`maxTurnRate` 360°/s exists because the recorded yaw is the CAMERA's.** A mouse flick turns thousands of degrees per second and uncapped that is one enormous phase jump — legs blurring through several strides in a tick. Capped, a 2000°/s flick tops out at 0.81 cycles/s. The cap is applied **per frame** (`maxTurnRate * dt`) so it holds at any frame rate, and the body still snaps round regardless: it is following the recording, only the feet stop keeping up.
    - `Mathf.DeltaAngle`, not a subtraction — yaw is an euler angle and a ghost crossing 0/360 would register a full spin in one frame. `lastYaw` is seeded in `ResetPlayback` alongside `lastPosition`, or the first tick after a reset reads the gap between where the ghost finished and where it restarts as a single turn.
  - **The other eleven clips in the FBX are unused, and `Man_Jump` is among them** — so jumping needs no download, only wiring. It was left alone deliberately: jumping is not a mechanic here (`EscapeTrigger` ignores Y on purpose), so it is only ever seen when a player is idling. **What genuinely has no clip is strafing** — a ghost walking sideways plays a forward stride. Backing up can be faked by scrubbing the phase negative; sideways needs directional clips and a 2D blend tree, which needs the FBX reimported as Humanoid. All three are cheaper to judge after a play-test than before, and the afterimage shader lowered the payoff on every one of them: a see-through silhouette sliding sideways is far less legible as wrong than an opaque person doing it.
  - Three states, no transitions (`Assets/Prefabs/GhostAnimator.controller`): **Walk** scrubbed by `distance / metresPerCycle`, **Idle** by the clock below `walkThreshold` (a scrubbed walk cycle stopped dead leaves the figure frozen mid-stride with its legs apart), and **Swing** for a recorded balloon pop. Speed is measured smoothed with hysteresis, since a ghost's position arrives from a scrubbed timeline in uneven steps.
  - A pop now takes the **whole body** for the length of the swing. The primitive ghost could throw one arm out while the rest kept walking; a rigged figure cannot be half-posed without an avatar mask and a second layer, and a ghost that stops to swing reads perfectly well.
  - **`AssetDatabase.LoadAllAssetsAtPath` returns the editor's hidden `__preview__` copies of every clip alongside the real ones, and they sort first.** Binding a state to one leaves an animator that works in the editor and has nothing to play in a build. It looked completely correct until the states were dumped and read — `AddGhostState` skips them explicitly.
