**Subject:** Asking permission — a puzzle game built on *Iteration 1*

Hi Jess and Lucas,

I'm Seongyo Kim, a final-year computer engineering student at Handong Global University in South
Korea, and I make games on my own. I've been building a first-person puzzle game based directly on
the premise of your 2016 short film *Iteration 1*, and I'd like to ask for your permission before
taking it any further.

Rather than simply saying the game was "inspired by" the film, I want to be clear about what comes
from your work. The white room, the sixty-second loop, dying and waking up in the same place again,
and above all, solving puzzles with the help of past versions of yourself — those are the film's
ideas. They are the reason this game exists. After watching *Iteration 1*, I wanted to experience
that idea as a game.

What I've added is the part that comes from my own interpretation. The past selves are recordings,
and each one has to re-check whether what it did still makes sense in the world as it exists now. A
past self might move an object, hand it to another past self, or fail because the player has already
taken the object it was supposed to use. The building, puzzles, and the rest of the game's structure
are my own.

Rather than ask you to take my word for it, here is two and a half minutes of it:

https://youtu.be/AAUtEWdRpis

The pale figures in it are those past selves.

Right now, the game is free, unfinished, and has been played by very few people. I'd like to finish
it and eventually release it on Steam. Before I do that, I thought it would be better to ask you
directly rather than simply assume it would be okay.

So, would you be comfortable with me releasing the game, including commercially, with *Iteration 1*
clearly credited as the work it is based on? If so, I'd be glad to know how you'd like to be
credited.

If you'd prefer me to move the game further away from the film, I'm happy to do so. I'd just rather
know now, before a store page exists.

Either way, thank you for making the film. It stayed with me.

Best regards,
Seongyo Kim

P.S. The working title is *Iteration Room*. If you feel that is too close to *Iteration 1*, I'm happy
to change it.

## Which link, and when to send

**One link, and it should be the easiest thing to look at.** That is now the video, and the itch.io
build is deliberately NOT in the mail: two links make a reader choose, and the choice costs more
than the second link adds. A director reads video fluently and owes this project nothing — the whole
point is to make looking cheaper than replying.

**Why YouTube unlisted rather than the Drive link originally planned.** A Drive link can prompt a
Google sign-in, serves a low-quality preview until its transcode finishes, and pushes some viewers
into downloading a 300MB file before they can watch. Unlisted YouTube plays on click, stays out of
search, and reports whether it was watched at all. Same rule as everything else here: the easiest
thing to look at.

**Send only once the 1080p60 processing has finished.** YouTube serves a soft, low-resolution version
for the first several minutes after upload, and a white room with fine black panel lines is close to
the worst case for that — the one impression that cannot be retaken.

The itch URL is not recorded anywhere in this repo, which is its own small problem — put it in the
README. It is no longer needed for this mail.

## Notes on sending it

- **Send it from a real address with your real name.** This is a permission record; an anonymous
  handle is worth less if it ever has to be produced.
- **Link, don't attach.** A build in an attachment gets a mail rejected or ignored. One link.
- **Don't send a legal document.** A licence agreement to sign converts a friendly yes into a
  decision he has to think about. An informal written yes in an email is worth having and is far more
  likely to arrive.
- **Keep whatever comes back**, including a casual one. The value is that it is *written*.
- **Follow up once**, two or three weeks later, then leave it. Silence is not permission — if nothing
  comes back, the fallback in `asset-licences.md` applies: keep the mechanism, change the expression.
  Cycle 3's rooms are already being built new, so that is the natural moment for Room1 and Room2 to
  stop being the film's rooms.
- **A "yes" is worth putting on the store page** — *"made with the permission of the director"* is a
  line most projects in this position cannot write.
